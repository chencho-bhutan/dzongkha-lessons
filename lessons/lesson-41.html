Using བྱ་ཚིག་གི་ཚིག་གྲོགས in Forming Past, Present, and Future Tenses

Back when we first met verbs, we noted that a verb on its own — like འགྱོ ("go") — doesn't tell you when something happens. Dzongkha handles this with a set of helper words that attach to a verb.

These helper words are called བྱ་ཚིག་གི་ཚིག་གྲོགས, and they do the same job as English words like "will," "is," and "-ed."

The Three Time Markers

  • ནི་འོང་གེ — used with a verb to show the future tense
  • ཅི་ཡི་ནུག — used with a verb to show the past tense
  • དོ་དེས — used with a verb to show the present tense

Examples

Past Tense

སླབ་ཅིhas said / said
བརྐོ་ཡིhas dug / dug
ཉལ་ནུགhas slept

Future Tense

བྱིན་ནིwill give
འབད་འོང་will do
འཁྱུ་གེwill wash

Present Tense

འགྱོ་དོam/is going
འབྲི་དེསis/are/am writing

Notice how each helper word attaches directly to the end of the verb — much like adding "-ed" or "-ing" does in English, except Dzongkha spells the whole addition out as its own syllable.
