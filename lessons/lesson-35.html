རྣམ་དབྱེ་གསུམ་པ — The Doer of an Action

This sentence type tells you about the subject of a verb — specifically, who or what did the action. It closely matches what's called "active voice" in English grammar (as in "Tashi cut the tree," rather than "the tree was cut").

Helping word used: ཀྱིས, གྱིས, གིས, འིས, ཡིས

Examples

འཕྲུལ་གླང་གིས་ཞིང་རྨོ།The tractor plows the field.
བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཀྱིས་ཤིང་བཏོགTashi cuts down the tree.
ནོར་གྱིས་རྩྭ་བཟའ།The cattle eat the grass.
བླ་མ་ཡིས་རབ་གནས་མཛད།The lama performs the consecration.
བླ་མ་གིས་རབ་གནས་མཛད།The lama performs the consecration. (a different form of the same sentence)

Two Kinds of "Doer"

Within this sentence type, there's a useful distinction between two kinds of doer:

The Primary Doer — བྱེད་པ་པོ་གཙོ་བོ

This is the real agent behind the action — a living being that is actually carrying it out.

བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཀྱིས་ཤིང་བཏོགTashi cuts down the tree.
ནོར་གྱིས་རྩྭ་བཟའ།The cattle eat the grass.
བླ་མ་ཡིས་རབ་གནས་མཛད།The lama performs the consecration.
བླ་མ་གིས་རབ་གནས་མཛད།The lama performs the consecration.

The Secondary Doer — བྱེད་པ་པོ་ཕལ་པ

Sometimes the "doer" in a sentence isn't a living being at all — it's a tool, machine, or instrument being used by someone else to carry out the action.

འཕྲུལ་གྱིས་ཤིང་བཏོགThe machine cuts down the tree.
ཁབ་ཀྱིས་བརྩེམ[It was] sewn with a needle.

Notice that grammatically, the machine and the needle are treated exactly the same way a person would be — they get the same helping word — even though they're not the "real" actor behind the action.
