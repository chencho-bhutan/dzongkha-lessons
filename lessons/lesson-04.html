Compounded Alphabets — མགོ་ཅན་ དང་ འདོགས་ཅན

So far, every letter you've seen has stood on its own, one after another in a line — the way English letters do. Dzongkha has another trick.

Some letters can stack directly on top of, or underneath, another letter, both belonging to the same single sound. These stacked combinations are called compounded alphabets, and there are two kinds:

  • Gochen (མགོ་ཅན) — letters with another letter stacked on top
  • Dogchen (འདོགས་ཅན) — letters with another letter attached underneath

Why Does Dzongkha Do This?

At first glance, stacking letters might seem like an unnecessary complication. But think of it this way: English does something similar with letter combinations like "sh," "ch," or "th" — two letters that combine to make one sound that neither letter makes alone. Dzongkha's stacking works on the same basic idea, except instead of writing the two letters side by side, it stacks them vertically into a single compact block.

Once you can recognize a handful of these stacked shapes, they stop looking unusual and start looking like exactly what they are: a normal, compact way of writing a specific sound.
