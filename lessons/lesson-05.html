The Three Gochen — མགོ་ཅན་གསུམ

Gochen (མགོ་ཅན) literally means "letters that have another letter on top of them."

There are exactly three letters that can sit on top of another letter this way: ར (ra), ལ (la), and ས (sa). Each one has its own specific list of base letters it's allowed to sit on top of.

Ra-go — Twelve Letters with ར on Top

ར་མགོ་བཅུ་གཉིས — "ra-go chunyi"

A Simple Way to Picture It

Each of these three letters — ར, ལ, and ས — has its own "guest list" of base letters it's allowed to sit on top of. Notice a few of the same base letters show up on more than one list (for example, ཀ appears under all three: རྐ, ལྐ, and སྐ). That's completely normal — a base letter can be visited by more than one "topper," just not by all of them, and not by any letter outside its specific guest list.

You don't need to memorize these thirty-three combinations by brute force right away. For now, it's enough to recognize that this stacking exists and which three letters do the stacking. Reading and pronunciation come next.
