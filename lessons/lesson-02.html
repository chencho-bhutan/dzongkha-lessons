The Thirty Alphabets — གསལ་བྱེད་སུམ་ཅུ

Every language starts somewhere, and for Dzongkha, it starts with thirty letters.

These letters are called Sel-jay (written གསལ་བྱེད), which simply means the building-block sounds of the language — the basic units everything else is made from. If you learn these thirty well, you've already unlocked the biggest single piece of reading and writing Dzongkha.

The Thirty Letters

A Helpful Way to Think About It

If you look closely at Rows 1–5, you'll notice a pattern: each row has a "light" version, a "breathy" version (said with a small puff of air), a "heavier" version, and a nasal version (said through the nose, like ng, n, or m). That's not a coincidence — Dzongkha, like many related languages, groups its letters by how your mouth and throat produce the sound, not just by how they look. Once your ear picks up on that pattern, remembering the thirty letters gets much easier, because you're really only learning a handful of mouth positions, repeated with small variations.
