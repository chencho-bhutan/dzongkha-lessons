Classes of Sentences — རྣམ་དབྱེ་བརྒྱད

Dzongkha and English share the same basic idea when it comes to building sentences: you join words, phrases, and clauses together using helping words — the same ones we covered in the last chapter.

Dzongkha organizes everyday sentences into eight general types, called རྣམ་དབྱེ་བརྒྱད. Between them, these eight types cover most of what people actually say and write day to day. They can also be combined or chained together to express more detailed or nuanced ideas.

Note: the source material for this course walks through seven of the eight types in detail — the eighth isn't covered yet. We'll add it once that content is available.

What Each Type Does

Each of the seven types we'll cover uses a specific helping word (usually one you've already learned) to signal a specific role in the sentence — the doer of an action, the thing being acted on, the purpose behind an action, and so on. Once you recognize the helping word, you immediately know what job that part of the sentence is doing.
