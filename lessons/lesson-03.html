The Four Vowels — དབྱངས་བཞི

Every one of the thirty letters you just learned already carries a hidden vowel sound.

Say ཀ (ka), ཏ (ta), or མ (ma) out loud — notice they all end in an "ah" sound? That "ah" is built into every letter by default. It's called the letter's inherent vowel.

To make other vowel sounds, Dzongkha doesn't add new letters — it adds small marks above or below the existing letter. These marks are called Yaang (written དབྱངས), and there are four of them.

How They Attach to a Letter

Take the letter ཀ (ka) as an example. Add each vowel mark, and the sound changes completely, even though it's still fundamentally "the ཀ letter":

With vowelReads as
ཀིki
ཀུku
ཀེke
ཀོko

This is the pattern for every single letter in the alphabet — all thirty of them take all four vowels the exact same way. Once you're comfortable with how vowels attach to ཀ, you already know how they'll behave with every other letter too.

Why This Matters

This is a genuinely efficient system once it clicks: instead of memorizing a completely separate written symbol for every vowel-consonant combination (the way some other scripts work), Dzongkha reuses the same four small marks on top of any of the thirty base letters. Learn four marks, and you can already sound out an enormous number of syllables.
