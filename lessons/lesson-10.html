Combining སྔོན་འཇུག, མིང་གཞི, རྗེས་འཇུག, and ཡང་འཇུག to Form Words

Now that you know the five possible roles a letter can play, let's look at how real Dzongkha words actually use them. Most words don't use all five at once — most use just two or three.

Pattern 1: Just the Main Letter

The simplest possible word is a single letter, doing nothing but being the Mingzhi.

ཁmouth
ངI
ཉfish
ཟeat
རgoat
ཤmeat
སsoil / land

Why This Matters

A word like བསྒྲུབས might look intimidating at first glance — five letters stacked and lined up together. But once you know to look for the four roles, it stops being a wall of symbols and becomes a small, readable sentence in itself: before-letter, main letter, after-letter, after-that-letter. That's the whole trick.

What You Should Be Able to Do Now

  1. Pick out the Ngonjug, Jejug, and Yangjug letters from the full alphabet
  2. Identify the Mingzhi — the letter that drives the word's sound
  3. Spot a vowel mark on the Mingzhi, if one is there
  4. Identify the Ngonjug, Jejug, and Yangjug in an actual word, if they're present
