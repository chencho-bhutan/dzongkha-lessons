How to Read and Pronounce the Gochen

Here's the most important thing to understand about Gochen letters: the top letter is silent. It's there in writing, but you don't say it out loud. Only the bottom letter's sound comes through.

The Traditional Way of "Spelling It Out"

Dzongkha has a traditional way of naming a stacked letter out loud before saying its final sound — almost like spelling a word out letter by letter in English before saying the whole word. Here's how it works for a few examples:

རྐ

Spelled out as "ra, ka, tak, ka" — meaning "ra on top of ka is ka." The final sound is just ka. The top letter ར is written, but it isn't heard.

ལྐ

Spelled out as "la, ka, tak, ka" — meaning "la on top of ka is ka." Again, the final sound is just ka.

སྐ

Spelled out as "sa, ka, tak, ka" — meaning "sa on top of ka is ka." The final sound is, once again, ka.

You'll notice all three examples land on the same sound: ka. That's the core idea of Gochen — no matter which of the three letters (ར, ལ, or ས) sits on top, the base letter underneath keeps its own sound, and the topper stays silent.

An Important Exception — Still Being Confirmed

For three specific base letters — ཀ, ཏ, and ཙ — the pronunciation stays exactly the same even when ར is stacked on top, exactly as described above.

However, for the other base letters in the Ra-go list, there's an additional detail about how the pronunciation changes that hasn't been fully confirmed yet. This section will be updated once that rule is confirmed.
